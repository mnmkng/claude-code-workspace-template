# Product

What Dunder Mifflin sells, how it is packaged and priced, and how clients order
it. Revenue shares and margins cited here come from `context/key-metrics.md`
with their as-of dates.

## Paper

61% of revenue, 19.1% gross margin (as of August 2026). The volume line and the
reason clients call us first.

### Grades and stock

| Grade | Typical use | Basis weight | Brightness | Packaging |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard copy | Everyday printing and copying | 20 lb | 92 | 10 reams per case |
| Premium copy | Client-facing documents, color printing | 24 lb | 98 | 10 reams per case |
| Bond | Letterhead, legal filings | 24 lb | 98 | 10 reams per case |
| Cover stock | Covers, tabs, presentation | 65-110 lb | 92-98 | 250 sheets per pack |
| Carbonless | Multi-part forms, invoices, delivery notes | 2, 3, and 4 part | - | 500 sets per case |
| Continuous forms | Legacy impact printers, still used by county offices | 15-20 lb | - | 2,000 sheets per case |
| Recycled | Sustainability requirements in public bids | 20 lb, 30% and 100% post-consumer | 84-92 | 10 reams per case |

Sizes stocked: letter, legal, ledger, and A4 on the standard and premium grades.
Everything else is letter and legal only, with other sizes on a converting order
at a 10-business-day lead time.

**A ream is 500 sheets. A case is 10 reams.** Quotes and invoices are in cases.

### Why paper margin is thin

Paper is a commodity with a published mill price, and every buyer can check it.
We buy from three mills and hold a Scranton-warehoused inventory; the margin is
the spread plus the delivery, and it compresses whenever mill prices move up
faster than we can reprice contracts. The business does not make money on paper
alone, which is what the attach lines are for.

## Office supplies

24% of revenue, part of the 38.6% blended margin on supplies and printer service
(as of August 2026). Toner and inkjet cartridges, filing and storage, writing
instruments, desk accessories, breakroom consumables, and janitorial supplies.

Supplies exist to make the account whole. A client who buys paper from us and
everything else somewhere else is a client with an active relationship with
another distributor - which is how accounts get taken. Attach rate on supplies
is a standing item in the quarterly account review.

Catalog is roughly 4,000 stock-keeping units, of which about 600 are warehoused
in Scranton and the rest ship from the supplier on a 3-to-5-day lead time.

## Printers and service

11% of revenue, the highest-margin line. Multifunction printer placement on a
36- or 48-month agreement, bundled toner, and on-site service through a
contracted regional service partner.

The strategic value is lock-in that the client is happy about: a placed printer
comes with a toner supply agreement, which means a standing order, which means
the account does not shop the paper line every quarter. Placements are the
single best predictor of retention in the CRM.

Service calls are answered by the partner, but the account representative owns
the outcome. A service failure on a placed printer is an account-level event,
not a ticket.

## Delivery

Included in the price within the branch delivery radius: roughly 45 minutes'
drive from Scranton, and the equivalent from Utica.

| Service | Commitment | Charge |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | Next business day, ordered before 3 p.m. ET | Included |
| Expedited | Same day, ordered before 10 a.m. ET, radius only | Flat fee |
| Scheduled standing order | A fixed day each week or month | Included |
| Out of radius | Third-party carrier, 2-3 days | Passed through at cost |

Next-day rate in Scranton is 96% (as of August 2026). Utica is not
instrumented and does not report a rate; do not infer one from Scranton's.

Delivery is made by our own drivers out of our own warehouse. It is the second
half of the service moat, and the reason the warehouse safety and accuracy
numbers are company KPIs rather than departmental ones.

## Infinity ordering

Dunder Mifflin Infinity is the client-facing ordering platform. Clients place
orders, see their contract pricing, repeat a previous order, track a delivery,
and download invoices.

| Capability | State |
|---|---|
| Catalog browse and search with contract pricing | Live |
| Reorder from order history | Live |
| Standing orders | Live |
| Delivery tracking | Live |
| Invoice download and payment | Live |
| Punch-out into a client's procurement system | Not built; the main blocker in the hospital segment |
| Mobile application | Not built; the site is responsive |

Infinity order share is 38%, and 51% among accounts under $25K a year (as of
August 2026). The target is 55% by Q4 2027.

**Infinity does not replace the phone.** An order placed through Infinity costs
less to process and reorders more predictably, which is why we move volume to
it. A client who wants to call still calls, and reaches their representative.
Removing that would remove the moat in order to save a few dollars of order
handling - the phone number stays on every invoice, every delivery note, and
every page of the platform.

## Pricing philosophy

1. **Price the account, not the order.** The quoted price reflects committed
   annual volume, not the size of the transaction in front of us. A large
   one-time order at a tier price sets an expectation we then have to defend.
2. **Discount is structural, not personal.** The volume tiers are published and
   the same for everyone. Anything beyond the tier is a negotiated exception
   with a named approver.
3. **Defend on service, not on price.** A competitor's headline price is met
   with a basket comparison and a named representative, not with a match.
4. **Hold the margin floor.** Below it, the deal does not get better with
   volume; it gets worse.
5. **The list price is real.** It is not an opening position to be discounted
   from as a matter of routine.

The tier table, the discount authority by role, and the deal desk rule live with
the sales department, in its `context/` folder, because the approval chain is a
sales process rather than company-wide knowledge.
