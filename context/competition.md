# Competition

Who we lose to, why, and what we say about it. Competitor pricing here is
collected from published lists and public bid awards only.

## The landscape

| Competitor | Type | Where we meet them | How we do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Big-box office retailers | National chains, retail plus a business delivery arm | Small business, and any account that shops on a single headline price | Lose on list price, win on service and on the total basket |
| Prince Family Paper | Independent regional distributor | Small business in and around Lackawanna County | Even; decided account by account |
| Mill-direct programs | Paper mills selling straight to very large buyers | Hospital systems, the largest districts | Rarely compete; below our volume floor |
| Regional janitorial and breakroom suppliers | Adjacent distributors expanding into paper | Supplies attach on existing accounts | Lose attach revenue quietly if nobody is watching the basket |

## Big-box office retailers

The category, not any one chain: national office retailers with a store
footprint and a business delivery arm. They are the default alternative for
every small-business prospect we call.

**Where they beat us**

- Published list price on high-volume commodity grades, typically 8-14% under
  our list.
- Same-day pickup at a store, which we cannot match at all.
- A self-service web experience with years more investment behind it than
  Infinity has.
- Procurement departments that already have them on an approved vendor list.

**Where they lose**

- Nobody answers for the account. A problem enters a queue, and the buyer
  repeats the story to three people.
- Headline pricing covers a few grades. The rest of the basket - cover stock,
  carbonless, specialty sizes, toner - is at or above our price, so a full
  basket comparison usually closes to within a few percent.
- Delivery is a national logistics network with a service-level agreement, not
  a local truck. Exceptions are handled badly.
- Stock-outs on specialty items, with a substitution made automatically rather
  than by agreement.

**How we position**

Compare the whole basket, never one grade. Ask what happened the last time an
order arrived wrong. Quote a named representative and a direct phone number as
a line item of the offer, because it is one. Never open with a discount to
match a headline price - that concedes the only frame in which we lose.

## Prince Family Paper

A family-owned independent distributor covering roughly our Scranton footprint.
Comparable size, comparable model, comparable service promise. The honest
assessment: they are us, with a longer local history in some townships and a
thinner supplies catalog.

**Where they beat us:** incumbency and local relationships in accounts they have
held for decades; a lower cost base, so they can hold a lower price longer.

**Where they lose:** no ordering platform, so accounts that want to place orders
without a phone call have nowhere to go; a narrower catalog, so a client
consolidating vendors has to keep a second supplier; no coverage in Utica.

**How we position:** on catalog breadth and on Infinity. Never on their service,
which is genuinely good, and never on their being a family business. A
representative who disparages them loses the room.

## Michael Scott Paper Company - the cautionary tale

A Scranton distributor founded in 2009 by a former regional manager of this
branch, staffed with people who left with him. It took real volume from the
Scranton branch inside a quarter, and the company acquired it later that year.
Its clients and two of its staff are ours now.

It is documented here because of how it took that volume, and the lesson still
applies:

- It undercut us on price on exactly the accounts where we were complacent -
  long-held accounts nobody had reviewed in years.
- It won on relationships that belonged to individual representatives, not to
  Dunder Mifflin. When the people left, the accounts followed.
- Its unit economics never worked. It was selling below cost and did not know
  it, which is why it could not be sustained and why the company could buy it.

**What we do about it:** quarterly account reviews on accounts over $25K a year,
so no account goes unreviewed long enough to be cheap to take; account records,
order history, and pricing live in the CRM rather than in a representative's
head; and every quote is checked against margin floors before it goes out - see
`pricing-and-discounts.md` in the sales department folder.

## Positioning summary

We are not the cheapest supplier and do not try to be. We sell a complete
catalog delivered next day by a local branch, with one named person accountable
for the account. That is worth a few percent over a big-box list price to a
buyer who has been burned, and worth nothing to a buyer who has not - which is
what qualification is for.

Three rules for competitive conversations:

1. Compare baskets, not line items.
2. Never name a competitor's client, price, or staff in writing.
3. If the only way to win is below the margin floor, the deal is not a win. See
   the deal desk rule in the sales department folder.
